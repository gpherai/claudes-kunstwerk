    २ · Nāda Brahma

    The world is sound

    ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वम्

    om ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvam

    Om — this syllable is all this. All that was, is and will be is Om; and whatever lies beyond the three times, that too is Om. Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1

    1. अ

      A · Jāgrat — waking

      The first sound opens the throat. It is the waking world: turned outward, shared by all, made of objects.

    2. उ

      U · Svapna — dreaming

      The second sound rolls forward through the mouth. It is the dream world: turned inward, luminous, made of mind.

    3. म

      M · Suṣupti — deep sleep

      The third sound closes the lips. It is dreamless sleep: undivided, at rest, a single mass of awareness.

    4. ॐ

      Turīya — the fourth

      Then, the silence after the sound. Not a state but the witness of all states — peaceful, auspicious, without a second. This, says the Upaniṣad, is the Self.

    ३ · Ṛg Veda 10.129

    The Hymn of Creation

    Some three thousand years ago, a Vedic poet asked the oldest question of all — and had the courage to leave it open.

    1. नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीं नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत् ।
      किमावरीवः कुह कस्य शर्मन्नम्भः किमासीद्गहनं गभीरम् ॥

      Then there was neither non-being nor being; no realm of air, no sky beyond. What stirred? Where? In whose keeping? Was there water, bottomlessly deep?

    2. न मृत्युरासीदमृतं न तर्हि न रात्र्या अह्न आसीत्प्रकेतः ।
      आनीदवातं स्वधया तदेकं तस्माद्धान्यन्न परः किं चनास ॥

      There was no death then, nor deathlessness; no sign of night or day. That One breathed, without wind, by its own nature. Other than that, there was nothing at all.

    3. तम आसीत्तमसा गूळ्हमग्रेऽप्रकेतं सलिलं सर्वमा इदम् ।
      तुच्छ्येनाभ्वपिहितं यदासीत्तपसस्तन्महिनाजायतैकम् ॥

      Darkness was hidden by darkness in the beginning; all this was formless water. What was coming into being, covered by the void — that One was born through the power of heat.

    4. कामस्तदग्रे समवर्तताधि मनसो रेतः प्रथमं यदासीत् ।
      सतो बन्धुमसति निरविन्दन्हृदि प्रतीष्या कवयो मनीषा ॥

      In the beginning, desire arose in it — the first seed of mind. Searching their hearts with wisdom, the poets found the bond of being in non-being.

    5. को अद्धा वेद क इह प्र वोचत्कुत आजाता कुत इयं विसृष्टिः ।
      अर्वाग्देवा अस्य विसर्जनेनाथा को वेद यत आबभूव ॥

      But who really knows? Who here can say it — where it was born, where this creation came from? The gods came later than this world's creation. So who knows where it arose?

    6. इयं विसृष्टिर्यत आबभूव यदि वा दधे यदि वा न ।
      यो अस्याध्यक्षः परमे व्योमन्त्सो अङ्ग वेद यदि वा न वेद ॥

      Where this creation came from — whether it made itself, or whether it did not — the one who watches over it in the highest heaven, surely he knows. Or perhaps he does not know.

    ४ · Kāla

    Time is a wheel

    Time here does not run in a line from creation to the end. It turns — in cycles within cycles, from the blink of an eye to the lifetime of the creator himself.

    निमेष

    Nimeṣa

    0.2 s

    1. Nimeṣa

      The blink of an eye. Fifteen blinks make a kāṣṭhā, thirty kāṣṭhās a kalā, thirty kalās a muhūrta.

    2. Muhūrta

      Forty-eight minutes. Thirty muhūrtas make a day and a night — and each has its own quality, favourable or not, for every undertaking.

    3. Divya varṣa

      A whole human year is a single day and night of the gods. Their year lasts 360 of ours.

    4. Yuga

      Four ages follow each other, shorter each time, in the ratio 4 : 3 : 2 : 1. In the first, Dharma stands firm on four legs like a bull; with every age it loses one.

      Satya1,728,000
      Tretā1,296,000
      Dvāpara864,000
      Kali432,000

    5. Mahāyuga

      Together the four ages form a great age of 4,320,000 years. Seventy-one great ages make the reign of one Manu, the father of humanity.

    6. Kalpa

      A thousand great ages are one day of Brahmā, the creator: 4.32 billion years — roughly the age of the Earth. An equally long night follows, in which the worlds rest, dissolved.

    7. Mahākalpa

      Brahmā lives a hundred years of such days and nights: 311 trillion years. Then he too dissolves — and a new Brahmā awakens on a lotus.

    8. And beyond?

      Countless universes, says the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, drift like specks of dust through the pores of the Infinite — each with its own Brahmā, each turning on its own wheel.

    ६ · Ānanda Tāṇḍava

    The dance of Śiva

    In a ring of fire, Śiva as Naṭarāja — Lord of Dance — dances the universe into being, sustains it and dissolves it, in one endless movement. The dance has five acts.

    1. १

      Sṛṣṭi · creation

      In his upper right hand, the ḍamaru: the hourglass drum. Its beat is the first pulse of time — from sound, creation unfolds.

    2. २

      Sthiti · preservation

      His lower right hand is raised in abhaya mudrā — “do not fear.” The dance sustains everything it creates.

    3. ३

      Saṃhāra · dissolution

      In his upper left hand, agni: the fire that ends every form, so that new forms can arise. The ring of flames is the cosmos itself, always burning.

    4. ४

      Tirobhāva · concealment

      His right foot presses down Apasmāra, the dwarf of ignorance — the veil of forgetting that hides our true nature.

    5. ५

      Anugraha · grace

      His left hand points, like an elephant’s trunk, to the raised foot: refuge and release. Freedom is within reach.

    6. CERN, 2004

      A two-metre Naṭarāja stands at CERN, the European laboratory for particle physics — a gift from India. Its plaque quotes the physicist Fritjof Capra: “For the modern physicists, then, Shiva’s dance is the dance of subatomic matter.”

    ७ · Śrī Yantra

    Geometry of the Goddess

    Nine interlocking triangles — four pointing up for Śiva, pure consciousness, and five pointing down for Śakti, creative energy — weave forty-three smaller triangles around a single point. The Śrī Yantra is worshipped as the body of the Goddess Tripura Sundarī: the whole cosmos, as geometry.

    1. १

      Bhūpura

      A square with four gates: the earth plane, where the pilgrim enters. Trailokya-mohana — enchanter of the three worlds.

    2. २–३

      Lotuses of 16 and 8 petals

      The fulfiller of all desires, and the stirrer of all — the senses and the mind, blossoming outward.

    3. ४

      Fourteen triangles

      Sarva-saubhāgya-dāyaka — the giver of all good fortune.

    4. ५–६

      Two rings of ten

      The accomplisher of all aims, and the protector of all.

    5. ७–८

      Eight triangles, then one

      The remover of all ills; then the single inner triangle, giver of every perfection.

    6. ९

      Bindu

      The point without dimension, where Śiva and Śakti are one. Sarvānanda-maya — made entirely of bliss. The journey inward ends where everything began.

    7. ॥

      Mahā Meru

      Raise every enclosure to the height of its rank and the diagram becomes a mountain: the Mahā Meru, the Śrī Yantra in three dimensions, cast in metal and worshipped in homes and temples. Its summit is the bindu. Move your cursor to walk around it.

    ८ · Kolam

    A line around every point

    Every dawn across Tamil Nadu, women sweep the threshold and draw a kolam: one flowing line of rice flour, looping around a grid of dots. By noon it is gone — walked over, blown away, eaten by ants and birds. That is the point: a daily gift of beauty, and of food for the smallest beings. Tomorrow, a new one.

    ९ · Mandira

    The temple is a mountain

    1. Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala

      It begins as a diagram: a grid of sixty-four squares on which the cosmic being lies, with Brahmā at its centre. The plan of the temple is a map of the universe.

    2. Garbhagṛha

      At the heart, the “womb-house”: a small, dark, unadorned chamber where the deity dwells. Everything else exists to lead you here.

    3. Śikhara

      Above the womb rises the “peak” — Mount Meru, axis of the world, the mountain at the centre of the cosmos.

    4. A fractal in stone

      Smaller peaks cluster around the great one, and smaller ones around those. At Khajuraho’s Kandāriya Mahādeva temple, eighty-four miniature spires echo the whole — here, too: one, four, sixteen, sixty-four. A thousand years before the word existed, a fractal.

    5. Pradakṣiṇā

      The pilgrim circles it clockwise, keeping the sacred always to the right — the way the Sun seems to travel across the sky.

    १० · Daśāvatāra

    Ten descents

    Whenever dharma declines, Viṣṇu descends. The ten classical avatāras come in an order many have compared to evolution: from fish to amphibian, to beast, to man-beast, to dwarf, to the full human being — and one still to come.

    ११ · Ṛg Veda 1.164.46

    One truth, many names

    एकं सद्विप्रा बहुधा वदन्ति

    ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti

    “Truth is one; the wise call it by many names.” For many Hindus, the gods are faces of one reality — Brahman — the way one light breaks into many colours. Choose a light to meet a face.

    १२ · Bhagavad Gītā

    The song on the battlefield

    Between two armies, a warrior lays down his bow. His charioteer, Kṛṣṇa, answers his despair in seven hundred verses — on action, death and freedom. The Gītā has eighteen chapters; the war it opens lasted eighteen days. Spin the chariot wheel to receive a verse.

    1. १३ · Īśa Upaniṣad

      ॐ पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदं पूर्णात्पूर्णमुदच्यते ।
      पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥

      That is whole. This is whole. From the whole, the whole arises. Take the whole from the whole — and the whole remains.

    2. Look closely: every sphere reflects every other. Each one holds the whole.

    3. Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7

      तत् त्वम् असि

      tat tvam asi

      You are That.

    4. ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः

      Peace within. Peace around. Peace beyond.

    There is no end. Only return.
